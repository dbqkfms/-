To see why, we need the two things a superconductor does.
First, below a certain temperature, its electrical resistance drops to exactly zero.
A current in a superconducting loop keeps flowing almost indefinitely, like a hockey puck on perfect ice.

Your phone charger, meanwhile, gets warm doing nothing.

Second, it pushes magnetic fields out of itself, which is called the Meissner effect.
Think of a bouncer throwing the magnetic field out of the club.
That's why magnets can float over one, and in many real superconductors a bit of field gets pinned inside, locking the magnet in midair.
Zero resistance and the bouncer are the two tests, and they're the whole case today.
