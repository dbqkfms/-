```text
So that's a Tesla doing a kickflip over a Semi truck.

And if your first thought was 'that's AI,' you're not alone.

Half of the internet is already asking an AI chatbot. But chatbots can sound sure of themselves, so let's check the physics ourselves.

It flips in mid-air and lands on its wheels, and I keep rewatching that landing.

Tony Hawk is in shambles.

And this one drives onto a ramp, rolls along it upside down, and drops back onto its wheels.

Here's what we know. Tesla posted this footage itself from its Roadster event on October 15th.

The physics checks out. The numbers are Tesla's own, and we haven't seen anyone measure them independently yet.

Which is a very 2026 problem. Real footage now has to prove it isn't AI.

Here's the problem. Tires only push against the road, and no road is throwing a car over a truck.

So Tesla stopped relying on the road.

To be fair, the last Roadster Musk took off-road is still orbiting the Sun.

So today, it's two cheat codes, one race car from 1978, and the reason the top version seats one person.

So why not just bolt in a bigger motor? Because tires only push back on the road, and the road pushes the car forward.

And a tire can only push as hard as its grip, which comes down to how hard it's pressed down and how grippy the surface is.

Picture sprinting on ice in socks. Your legs give it everything, and you just vibrate aggressively in place.

That's not a skill issue. That's a floor issue.

Cars have the same problem. Too much power without enough grip just makes smoke.

Press the car down harder, though, and that limit goes up. Racers call that downforce.

Now freeze on my favorite frame, the car halfway over that Semi.

Up there, the tires aren't touching anything, so there's nothing to grip.

So Tesla needed two cheat codes. One presses the car down harder. The other pushes without the road at all.

Cheat code one. The car rolls onto the ramp and keeps going upside down. Why doesn't it fall off?

Tesla's page lists a fan under the car and a seal around the gap underneath.

The fan pulls air out from under the car. Now there's less air pushing up from below than pushing down from above.

And here's the part most people get backwards. The fan isn't really pulling the car down.

The fan just gets some air out of the way. The atmosphere does the shoving.

It's a suction cup phone mount. You don't pull it onto the windshield. The air outside presses it there.

Wait. It's the air doing the work?
```
